04

Validated twice, with opposite outcomes

Synthetic corpus

458k payments, ring-labelled, benign confounders planted

+24.5%

recall at precision 0.95. Survives a permutation test, 20% of fraud going unlabelled, and six leakage checks.

IEEE-CIS (Vesta)

590k real card-not-present transactions, 3.5% fraud

No difference

0.4546 ± 0.0043 against 0.4561 ± 0.0041 across five seeds. That gap is 0.4 pooled standard deviations.

A single-seed run had the graph ahead by +2.9% and I nearly wrote it up. Refitting across five seeds showed run-to-run variance was three times the size of the effect. There is now a seedstudy command, so no claim in the repo can be made without clearing its own noise floor.

I then offered an explanation for the null, that IEEE-CIS fraud is single-actor rather than collusive, and tested it. If true, the advantage should grow with how much linked-account structure a payment sits in. Bucketed by cluster size there is no trend, and the only significant result is the graph doing worse on small clusters. The explanation was withdrawn.

The graph produces a large, verified improvement on a benchmark whose ring structure I constructed. It produces no measurable improvement on real data, and a targeted search for the subgroup where it should have helped found nothing. Why it does not transfer is unresolved.
05

How it works

Three decisions carry the result.

1

Shared identifiers are graded, not binary

Most identifier sharing is innocent. Families share cards, a carrier NAT puts 400 customers on one IP, an apartment block shares a delivery address. Edge weight is prior[type] × log(N/df) / log(N), so a card on three accounts is loud and an IP on four hundred is silent. Hub identifiers past a per-type cap are dropped outright rather than down-weighted, because a carrier NAT block carries no information at any weight and keeping it injects an 80,000-edge clique.

2

Cohort synchrony separates a drop address from an apartment block

Both are twenty accounts on one address, structurally identical in a graph. What separates them is when the accounts were created. A building's residents signed up over years; a mule cohort signed up the same week. Without this feature the graph actively hurt bust-out recall.

3

Resolve by identifier, not by customer

A mule's first payment has no history, so a customer-keyed lookup returns nothing for exactly the transactions that matter most. Resolving through the drop address it ships to lifted fraud coverage from about 40% to 60%, and flipped the ablation from −4.6% to +23.9%. This one change is the difference between the method working and not.

06

Five times a number looked fine and was wrong

Each of these is written up in FINDINGS.md with the diagnostic reasoning, not just the fix.

Broken benchmark
A PR-AUC of 0.99

Not a good model, a broken benchmark. Every ring account was freshly minted and almost every honest account was aged, so account age alone separated them.

Wrong direction
The graph made the model worse

Snapshots ran on a 5-day stride and card-testing rings live 2 days, so the graph block became a tenure signal that anti-correlated with fraud.

Config destroying signal
Louvain was shattering the cliques

At resolution above 1 it splits a clique into singletons, and a ring sharing one drop address is a clique. The tell was a recall cliff at exactly the code's cutoff constant.

Silent leak
A pruned identifier still resolved clusters

Every payment carrying a gmail address inherited whatever cluster gmail touched: 100% of rows "in a cluster", median cluster of 153 accounts. Fixing it raised the headline.

Caught before publishing
A +2.9% lift that was seed noise

Refitting across five seeds showed run-to-run variance was three times the effect. Nearly became a reported result.

Self-refuted
My explanation for the null did not survive

I predicted the advantage should grow with cluster size, tested it, found no trend, and withdrew the explanation rather than keeping the comfortable story.

07

Every alert is contestable

"The model said so" is not something to put in front of a merchant whose payment you just blocked.

Reasons come from group occlusion rather than SHAP: each feature group is replaced with what a normal customer looks like and the payment re-scored, which gives a real counterfactual on the real model. Every alert also carries the subgraph it came from, with identifier values masked so the packet can travel into a ticket.

ringfence · analyst console

The queue ranks by money at risk, not raw score. Sorting on score alone returned 392 card-testing probes out of 400, every one tied at 0.9998 and worth about five rupees: a correct ranking and a useless product. The console also ships the worst false positives, because a model that can only explain its wins is not auditable.

No GNN, no LLM, no SHAP. Each was considered and rejected on grounds worth defending. A graph neural network overfits 4,500 positives and leaks future edges through neighbourhood aggregation, which would have destroyed the causality guarantees. An LLM is slower, costlier and non-deterministic for detection over structured data at scale. Classical graph algorithms engineer the features; gradient boosting makes the call.

09

Defense only

The track disqualifies anything offense-capable, and this is enforced by architecture rather than promised. The service consumes transaction records and emits risk scores and evidence. Every route is GET. There is no endpoint that blocks a payment, issues a refund, or mutates an account. The generator produces labelled abuse patterns for evaluation only: card fingerprints are opaque random tokens with no BIN structure, and no real cardholder data enters the system.
